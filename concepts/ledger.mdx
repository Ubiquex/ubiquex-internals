title: "Ledger"
description: "The per-stack, append-only chain of accepted proposals — the only source of truth."
---

The ledger is where a stack's accepted [proposals](/concepts/proposal)
live: a per-stack, append-only chain, stored in git (`ledger/` plus a
`ledger.lock` head pointer). It is the sole source of truth for what
infrastructure a stack owns. There is no `.tfstate`, no separate state
backend, no snapshot anywhere in the system — "current infrastructure" is
defined as the fold of every applied proposal in the chain, computed on
demand, not cached in a mutable file that something could silently
overwrite.

## Why append-only

A mutable state file can only ever answer "what exists right now." It
can't answer who decided a resource should exist, what they were solving
for, or what changed three revisions ago, because each write erases the
one before it. An append-only ledger keeps every proposal that was ever
accepted, permanently. `ubx why` on any resource walks the real chain
back to its origin — not a reconstruction from logs or commit messages,
a direct read of what's actually stored.

## Acceptance binds an approval to a hash

A proposal joins the ledger only once it's accepted, and acceptance binds
a recorded approval to the proposal's own content hash. Nothing is
cryptographically signed today; the record says how approval was obtained
(`acceptance.method`) and by whom, at whatever strength that channel can
attest:

- `local`: someone ran `ubx accept` in their own shell. The approver is
  the OS username, which is weak evidence and recorded as such.
- `loopback`: someone clicked approve on the local MCP server's page. The
  approver is always the fixed string `loopback:presence`, because the
  channel proves someone at the machine clicked, and nothing about who.

Older ledgers can also hold `pr_merge` and `pr_review` acceptances, from
when approval could be derived from a merged or approved pull request.
Both methods were removed with the pull-request workflow (UBI-300). The
records stay readable, with every field they wrote (platform, merge SHA,
pull request number, approvers, review comment), because a record that
silently lost those on read would no longer say what happened.
`ubx verify` reports them as from a removed method, neither verified nor
failed. Until a hosted service holds identity, no remaining method names
an approver: `local` records an unverified username and `loopback`
records presence.

A `loopback` acceptance also carries `rendered_hash`: the SHA-256 of the
exact bytes the approver was shown, produced by a deterministic renderer
(`core.RenderProposal`) and stored in the ledger store under
`rendered/<hash>.render` before the acceptance is written. When an agent
authored the proposal, its intent summary is the agent's own words; the
render is what answers "what did this person actually see."

The binding is what makes `parent` (the previous ledger head hash, carried
in every proposal) meaningful: it's not just a linked-list pointer, it's
proof the proposal was resolved against a specific, named point in the
chain's own history, and if that point has moved since, the proposal is
stale.

## One ledger per stack, referenced across stacks

Each stack owns its own ledger. A [cross-stack reference](/concepts/cross-stack-references)
from one stack into another pins against the neighbor's ledger head at
resolve time — it never reads a live, mutable pointer into a sibling's
state, because there isn't one; there's only the neighbor's own ledger,
folded the same way this stack's own would be.
